The Little Boy in the Village Called WhatsApp

A story about pain, hope, and the things we don’t say out loud.

A conversation that stayed

I had a conversation with a little boy who lives in a small village called WhatsApp. A boy who had been laughed at, misunderstood, and made to feel as though nothing good could come out of him.

It took a while before he opened up. When he finally did, he said, “Nobody understands how I feel. They think I just like talking. They don’t know I’m going through a lot.”

Sometimes, the people who talk the most are carrying things they cannot tell anyone. People see the jokes and personality, but may never see what happens when the laughter ends.

The weight he carries

He no longer wanted to tell people anything. Not because he had nothing to say, but because he was tired of explaining himself to people who had already made up their minds about him. He just wanted to be there, because we can never completely wear another person’s shoes.

He was tired, confused, frustrated, and losing the desire to try again. Perhaps he had not stopped wanting a better life; perhaps he was exhausted from wanting one and not knowing how to get there.

“Hope? Maybe.”

When I asked him about hope, he said, “Hope? Maybe.” 🥹

For years, his parents had been feeding him with hope. They were still hoping for him, even now. I wondered what it must feel like to have people believe so much in the person you could become while you’re struggling to believe in that person yourself.

You can love your parents, appreciate their sacrifices, and still feel exhausted. You can be grateful for their hope and still be honest about your pain.

The things he keeps private

He told me that he doesn’t have complete freedom to make certain decisions for himself, and that some of these struggles began when he was very young.

There were many things he shared that I won’t mention here. I’ll simply call them secrets. Not every experience needs to become public knowledge before someone deserves compassion.

Why AI became a place to talk

He preferred sharing his pain with AI. Then he said something that made me laugh and think at the same time: “All you need to know about me? Just meet my AI.” 😂😫

Beneath the humour was something painful. Imagine having so much inside you that one of the places you feel comfortable expressing yourself isn’t even a person.

Sometimes people need somewhere to put their thoughts without worrying about being judged. Still, everyone deserves safe and understanding human support, too.

Faith, fear, and surrender

He felt he might need to surrender everything to the One he knows as his Creator. But even surrendering scared him.

Perhaps faith doesn’t always mean having all the answers. Perhaps sometimes it means admitting you don’t understand while still seeking God. Surrender can mean accepting what you cannot control while taking small steps toward what you can.

Maybe that is enough for today

I don’t know what the future holds for this little boy, and I can’t promise that everything will suddenly become beautiful. But his current struggles are not the entirety of his story. His mistakes, disappointments, and other people’s opinions do not tell the whole story.

You don’t have to understand every detail of someone’s life to treat them with dignity. You don’t have to have all the answers to listen. And you don’t have to fix someone’s problems to remind them that they are not a problem themselves.

Behind the jokes, silence, frustration, secrets, and conversations with AI, there is a human being trying to make sense of his life.

And when asked about hope? “Hope? Maybe.” 🥹❤️

Maybe that’s all he can manage today. And maybe, for today, we can let that be enough.
